Braised Heirloom Short Ribs

Ingredients

  • 5 lbs Beef short ribs, bone-in
  • 2 tbsp Kosher salt
  • 1 tsp Black pepper
  • 3 tbsp Grapeseed oil
  • 2 large Yellow onions, diced
  • 3 large Carrots, peeled, chopped
  • 2 stalks Celery, finely sliced
  • 4 cloves Garlic, smashed
  • 2 tbsp Tomato paste
  • 750 ml Dry red wine (Cabernet)
  • 2 cups Beef bone broth
  • 3 sprigs Fresh rosemary

Chef's Notes

For the best results, sear the ribs until a deep brown crust forms on all sides. This Maillard reaction is essential for the depth of the final sauce. If prepping ahead, this dish tastes significantly better the following day after the flavors have had time to fully integrate. Chill overnight and remove the solidified fat layer before reheating gently on the stovetop. Pair with a creamy polenta or buttery mashed potatoes.

Instructions

01

Preheat your oven to 325°F (165°C). Pat the short ribs completely dry with paper towels. Season aggressively with kosher salt and black pepper on all sides. In a heavy Dutch oven, heat grapeseed oil over medium-high heat until shimmering.

02

Working in batches, sear the ribs for 3-5 minutes per side until deeply browned. Remove ribs and set aside. Reduce heat to medium; add onions, carrots, and celery. Sauté until softened and starting to caramelize, about 8 minutes.

03

Stir in garlic and tomato paste. Cook for 2 minutes until the paste darkens. Deglaze the pan with red wine, scraping up all the browned bits (fond) from the bottom. Simmer until the wine reduces by half.

04

Return the ribs and any accumulated juices to the pot. Add beef broth until the ribs are 3/4 submerged. Add rosemary sprigs. Bring to a bare simmer on the stovetop, then cover with a tight-fitting lid.

05

Transfer to the oven and braise for 3 to 3.5 hours, or until the meat is fork-tender and falling off the bone. Remove ribs carefully; strain the braising liquid through a fine-mesh sieve and reduce on the stovetop if a thicker sauce is desired.

06

Serve immediately over a base of root vegetable puree, spooning the glossy reduction over the meat. Garnish with a final pinch of flake salt and fresh herbs.
